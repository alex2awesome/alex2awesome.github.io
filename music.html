Music AI Research

I am very excited for the Aria project, a 3-year collaboration with Louis Bradshaw, funded by EleutherAI, to bear fruit! The goal of Aria was to train state-of-the-art generative music models. As a highlight, a Disklavier demo video is shown:

On Oct. 23, 2025, I gave an overview of the Aria project and its relation to linguistics in a talk at the Stanford NLP Lunch [slides].

We pretrain autoregressive transformers on roughly 60,000 hours of transcribed solo piano, then finetune on a small, high-quality subset. The generative model produces continuations that beat prior symbolic systems and compete with proprietary audio models; a contrastive variant yields MIDI embeddings that set the state of the art on MIR classification, often from a few hundred labeled examples.

These are the models behind the Disklavier demo above.

None of this works without transcription: the pretraining corpus is audio recordings turned into MIDI. Aria-MIDI, Louis's ICLR 2025 dataset, used a language model to crawl and score piano recordings and an audio classifier to prune them, transcribing about 100,000 hours into over a million MIDI files.

Transcription models trained only on clean studio audio, though, fall apart on the noise and reverb of real recordings. Our transcription work folds unlabeled audio into training to make them robust, and releases the models, re-transcriptions of the standard expressive MIDI datasets and 5,000+ new piano transcriptions.

Musicians have been slow to adopt generative composition tools, and I don't think it is because they object to sharing creative control: text-prompting is simply nothing like playing. Aria-Duet puts the model on a Yamaha Disklavier for a live, turn-taking duet. You play, signal a handover, and the model's continuation is performed acoustically on the same piano.

Musicologically, the model holds stylistic semantics across genres and develops coherent phrasal ideas rather than merely continuing. The same questions run through the ICML 2026 workshop on human-AI co-creativity that I co-organized.
